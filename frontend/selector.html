      “Measured in paper” is what the authors measured at 25 °C and 1 bar. “Predicted” is the model's estimate at your conditions, with the range in which the true value is expected to fall. Where the two can be compared (25 °C, 1 bar), they show how far the model can be off.

      How to read the results
      • CO₂ it can hold: how much CO₂ one gram of the carbon is expected to take up, in mmol (1 mmol/g is about 44 g of CO₂ per kg of carbon). The grey “likely” numbers give the range the real value should fall in.
      • Good match: the best estimate reaches your target (check the range: the real value can be a bit lower). Maybe: the best estimate is just below your target, but the range reaches it. Unlikely match: even the top of the range falls short.
      • “Heated to 800 °C” is how the carbon was made. It is not the temperature you entered.
      • Less certain: the study did not report every detail, so typical values were used for the missing ones.
      • Measured in paper: what the authors measured at 25 °C and 1 bar. Compare it with the prediction only if you entered 25 °C and 1 bar.
      • Some data missing: not every property was reported, so typical training values were used (shown to users as “Less certain”).

        Where these numbers come from, and what they can't tell you

          Please read: the model was trained on lab measurements of activated carbons at 0–30 °C and 0.1–1 bar, so results outside that are flagged as extrapolation. A carbon's properties depend on how it is made, not only on the biomass, so use this to shortlist what to test, not as a promise of performance. It does not cover cost, availability, speed of adsorption, repeated use, or humid and hot flue gas. “Not reported” values in a paper are replaced by typical training values, which is flagged as “Less certain” in the results.
