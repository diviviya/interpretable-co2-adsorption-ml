SCIENTIFIC CONTEXT

Methodology

A concise guide to the inputs, prediction output and interpretation used in CarbonLab.

What the platform predicts

CarbonLab estimates the CO₂ uptake of a porous carbon material under specified material properties and operating conditions. The output is expressed in millimoles of CO₂ per gram of adsorbent (mmol/g).

Input parameters

Textural properties

Specific surface area, total pore volume and micropore volume describe the accessible surface and pore structure of the adsorbent.

Elemental composition

Carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen, oxygen and sulfur percentages describe the material's bulk elemental composition.

Operating conditions

Temperature and pressure specify the conditions under which the adsorption uptake is estimated.

How to read a prediction

The predicted value is an estimate produced by the currently active model. It should be interpreted within the scope and limitations of the data used to develop and validate that model. It is not a direct experimental measurement.

Understanding SHAP explanations

SHAP values describe how input features contribute to a model output relative to a reference prediction. Positive contributions move the estimate higher; negative contributions move it lower. The size of a contribution indicates its influence on that prediction.

SHAP explains the model's learned associations. It does not establish physical causation or prove that changing a feature experimentally will produce the same change.

Important limitations

  • Predictions are estimates, not substitutes for experimental measurements.
  • Reliability depends on how well the input material and operating conditions are represented in the model's training data.
  • Extrapolation beyond the data domain may be unreliable.
  • Missing input values may be handled by the model's preprocessing pipeline, but measured values are preferable whenever available.
